Persuade, inform, or entertain

Students sort three short made-up texts using persuade, inform, and entertain, a purpose set published by Common Sense as P.I.E.

Today’s lesson

What is the main purpose of a media text?

Learning intentions

  • We are learning to name the main purpose of a short text.
  • We are learning that a text can do more than one job, and we can still name the main one.

I can statements students see

  • I can sort a text as mainly persuade, inform, or entertain.
  • I can point to a clue in the words.

Success criteria / evidence of learning

  • Sorts all three texts with a defensible main purpose.
  • Gives a clue from the text, not a guess about a real brand.

Materials and prep

  • This page.
  • Three headings on the board.
  • Timer.

No Chromebooks, no student logins, and no device project. A projector is optional. The page is the lesson.

Room or space setup

  • Read the attribution sentence so students know P.I.E. comes from the Common Sense lesson.

Lesson sequence (timed)

The four parts total 30 minutes: 5 + 8 + 12 + 5.

  1. Opening question · 5 min

    Say this. Why might someone make a poster? Take three purposes if you can: to teach, to sell, or to amuse. We will use those ideas today.

    Do this. Write persuade, inform, entertain. Say: Common Sense calls this P.I.E. Persuade, inform, entertain.

  2. Explain and model · 8 min

    Say this. Persuade means the creator wants you to believe or do something. Inform means the creator wants you to know a fact. Entertain means the creator wants you to enjoy it. Some texts do two. We name the main one and we can mention a second. Confirm the purpose from the text, not from the loudest picture alone.

    Do this. Model: “Bring a hat. It is sunny at recess.” Main purpose: inform. A small persuade sits inside “bring.”

  3. Guided scenario · 12 min

    Say this. Sort the three made-up texts. Write the main purpose and one clue. If you see a second purpose, add it after.

    Do this. Pairs sort. Review all three so errors do not sit. Accept entertain as a second purpose for the ad only if persuade is main.

  4. Check and close · 5 min

    Say this. What is the main purpose of the shoe sentence, and what is your clue?

    Do this. Preview role models and creators who persuade for a living.

If time is short. Sort text 1 together. Pairs do texts 2 and 3 only.

If you have extra time. Ask who the shoe sentence is for. Look for: children who play sports, or anyone who might buy shoes. That is a target audience, in original words: the people the creator hopes will pay attention.

Made-up scenario

Three short texts

Text 1: “Buy SwiftStride shoes and you will be the star.” Text 2: “Tomorrow’s forecast for our town: snow in the morning, sun after lunch.” Text 3: “The cat wore socks and applied to be the mayor.”

  • What is the main purpose of each?
  • What clue did you use?
  • Does any text have a second purpose?

Teacher answer guidance

  • Text 1 is mainly persuade. Clue: “buy” and the promise of being the star. It may also try to entertain, but selling is the main job.
  • Text 2 is mainly inform. Clue: a forecast someone could check.
  • Text 3 is mainly entertain. Clue: a cat as mayor is playful, not a claim to act on.
  • If a student calls text 1 inform because shoes exist, agree that a product is real-world, then return to the sentence’s job: it tells you to buy.

Quick check. What is the main purpose of the shoe sentence, and what is the clue?

Look for. Persuade. The clue is “buy” or the promise that you will be the star.

Tech-free Plan B

Read the three texts. The class holds up 1, 2, or 3 fingers for persuade, inform, or entertain.

Support

Leave a colour-free legend: P persuade, I inform, E entertain.

Extension

Rewrite the weather line so its main purpose becomes entertain, without losing the fact. The extension stays inside the block. It is not homework and not a project.

Safety notes

Physical activity: not applicable. There is no gym task and no equipment to set out.

No real ads that single out a student’s family or body.

Do not ask students for real names, addresses, schools, photos, usernames, passwords, screen-time diaries, or upsetting personal experiences. If a student discloses harm, follow the school’s safeguarding process. Do not investigate in front of the class.

Differentiation and inclusive supports

A student may match prepared purpose cards to the texts.

Follow ISP supports listed in the class binder. Do not write medical details or student names into this public site or onto a slide you will publish.

Classroom management cues

Do not let brand loyalty take over. The texts are made up.

Assessment

Three defensible sorts meet the intention. Swapping the weather and the joke is a sign to re-teach inform versus entertain with the model. Feedback quotes their clue. Reassess quickly at the start of Lesson 13 if needed.

Use observations, conversations, and products linked to the success criteria. Feedback describes what you noticed and one next step. There is no numeric or percent grade. If you use Elk Island Public Schools’ Grades 3–6 descriptive levels, the words are Not Yet Meeting Expectations, Minimally Meeting Expectations, Solidifying Expectations, and Mastering Expectations. A student can show the skill again in a flex lesson or in a one-minute conversation. Do not file personal stories.

Exit and clean-up

Leave the three purpose words up if the next class is yours. Otherwise erase student examples that used real brands.

Sub notes

The answer key is on this page. Accept a second purpose when the main one is right.

The say-this lines are the lesson. Keep the 5, 8, 12, and 5 timing. Skip the extension if the clock is tight.
