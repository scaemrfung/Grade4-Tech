Quick teaching steps · 30 minutes
  1. Before class: Open the PowerPoint or Canva presentation. Read the scenario and answer guidance below. If using an official video or handout, sign in and preview it first.
  2. Opening · 5 minutes: Show the title and big question. Invite made-up examples only; students may pass.
  3. Explain · 8 minutes: Use Learn It and model the safe move. If using a video, fit it into this block rather than adding time.
  4. Activity · 12 minutes: Use Try It Together, then Talk About It. Follow the script and let pairs think before showing answer guidance.
  5. Close · 5 minutes: Use Exit Check. Listen for the success criteria and record a short observation.

No projector? Read the same scenario aloud and use the tech-free Plan B below. No student devices or accounts are needed.

For students

Today’s lesson

What are thinking traps, and how can they change a feeling?

We are learning

  • We are learning to notice a thinking trap.
  • We are learning that a fairer thought can change the next step.

I can

  • I can spot the trap in the made-up story.
  • I can offer a fairer thought that is still believable.

What to do

  • Answer the opening question with a tiny made-up example, or pass.
  • Read Remy’s thought.
  • Name the trap, a fairer thought, and a next step.

Remy and the quiet chat

This story is made up.

Remy sends a made-up friend, “Want to trade stickers tomorrow?” Ten minutes pass with no reply. Remy thinks, “They saw it and ignored me, so nobody likes me.” The friend is at supper in the story, and Remy does not know that.

Curriculum outcomes

Host subject

Physical Education and Wellness, Grade 4

Organizing idea. “Character Development: Exploration of life opportunities and virtues develops resilience and personal talents and promotes lifelong learning.”

Guiding question. “How can a variety of life experiences influence resilience and perseverance?”

Learning outcome. “Students interpret how resilience and perseverance can be influenced by a variety of life experiences.”

Knowledge. “Strategies that support resilience include identifying a purpose, seeking positive role models, focusing on the solution instead of the challenge, breaking down tasks into smaller, achievable goals.”

Understanding. “Resilience may result in the increased ability to respond to future adversity. Resilience is supported by development of perseverance over time.”

Skills and procedures. “Describe strategies that support resilience.”

The outcome does not name technology or screen time. This lesson uses a made-up tech moment so students can describe a resilience strategy: focusing on the solution instead of the challenge.

General outcome. “Students will understand the role of technology as it applies to self, work and society.”

Specific outcome. “2.4 assess the personal significance of having limitless access to information provided by communication networks, such as the Internet”

Information and Communication Technology is infused into the host subject. The class is working toward the Division 2 outcome below. This 30-minute block does not achieve it. Learning intentions

  • We are learning to notice a thinking trap.
  • We are learning that a fairer thought can change the next step.

I can statements students see

  • I can spot the trap in the made-up story.
  • I can offer a fairer thought that is still believable.

Success criteria / evidence of learning

  • Identifies the trap: no reply yet does not prove nobody likes the character.
  • Offers a fairer thought tied to an ordinary reason, plus a calm next step.

Materials and prep

  • This page.
  • Board: Trap / Fairer thought / Next step.
  • Timer.

No Chromebooks, no student logins, and no device project. A projector is optional. The page is the lesson.

Room or space setup

  • Tell students this is not a time to share worries about real friendships. Pass is welcome. You will not ask who feels left out.

Lesson sequence (timed)

The four parts total 30 minutes: 5 + 8 + 12 + 5.

  1. Opening question · 5 min

    Say this. If you do not know why something happened, does your mind sometimes fill the gap with a story? Thumbs, and keep the example tiny and made up, such as a late bus.

    Do this. Affirm that brains guess. Sometimes the guess is a trap.

  2. Explain and model · 8 min

    Say this. A thinking trap is a negative thought that feels true and makes a situation seem worse than the facts show. The feeling follows the story. A resilience move is to focus on the solution instead of the challenge: name the trap, pick a fairer thought, choose one small next step. We will not dig into real fears. If a feeling is heavy, you may tell me privately or pass.

    Do this. Model the late bus: trap, “I will be in trouble forever.” Fairer thought: “The bus is late. I can tell an adult what happened.”

  3. Guided scenario · 12 min

    Say this. Look at Remy’s chat. Find the trap, a fairer thought, and one next step. Do not offer a real time someone left you on read.

    Do this. Pairs talk. Hear two fairer thoughts. Reject thoughts that are harsh in the other direction, such as “I do not need anyone.”

  4. Check and close · 5 min

    Say this. What is the trap in Remy’s story?

    Do this. Say Lesson 17 will be practice, like a short workout for this skill.

If time is short. Model Remy together. Collect only the fairer thought.

If you have extra time. Give a second trap: “I lost one game, so I am hopeless at floor hockey.” Fairer: “I lost today. I can practise one skill.”

Made-up scenario

Remy and the quiet chat

Remy sends a made-up friend, “Want to trade stickers tomorrow?” Ten minutes pass with no reply. Remy thinks, “They saw it and ignored me, so nobody likes me.” The friend is at supper in the story, and Remy does not know that.

  • What is the trap?
  • What is a fairer thought?
  • What is one calm next step?

Teacher answer guidance

  • The trap is treating a ten-minute wait as proof that nobody likes Remy.
  • A fairer thought: “They might be busy. One quiet chat is not the whole friendship.”
  • A next step: wait until tomorrow and ask at school, or send one calm “No rush” later. Not a string of new messages, and not a mean post.

Quick check. What is the thinking trap in Remy’s story?

Look for. Remy treats no reply yet as proof that nobody likes them.

Tech-free Plan B

The story is oral. Students show the trap by pointing to the sentence “so nobody likes me.”

Support

Two fairer thoughts are on a card. The student chooses the more believable one and says why.

Extension

Add the next step Remy could take tomorrow at school, still without blaming the friend. The extension stays inside the block. It is not homework and not a project.

Safety notes

Physical activity: not applicable. There is no gym task and no equipment to set out.

Stop and narrow the talk if students begin personal disclosures. Offer to listen later. Do not turn the lesson into counselling.

Do not ask students for real names, addresses, schools, photos, usernames, passwords, screen-time diaries, or upsetting personal experiences. If a student discloses harm, follow the school’s safeguarding process. Do not investigate in front of the class.

Differentiation and inclusive supports

A student who is anxious may do the bus model only and skip Remy. Note that as an adaptation, not as a fail.

Follow ISP supports listed in the class binder. Do not write medical details or student names into this public site or onto a slide you will publish.

Classroom management cues

No “who has no friends?” Keep voices ordinary. Thank passing.

Assessment

Trap plus a believable fairer thought meets the intention. A fairer thought that insults the friend does not. Feedback: “You found the jump from no reply to nobody likes me. That jump is the trap.” Reassess in Lesson 17.

Use observations, conversations, and products linked to the success criteria. Feedback describes what you noticed and one next step. There is no numeric or percent grade. If you use Elk Island Public Schools’ Grades 3–6 descriptive levels, the words are Not Yet Meeting Expectations, Minimally Meeting Expectations, Solidifying Expectations, and Mastering Expectations. A student can show the skill again in a flex lesson or in a one-minute conversation. Do not file personal stories.

Exit and clean-up

Erase the trap sentence so the fairer thought is the last line, then clear personal names if any appeared.

Sub notes

Do not probe feelings. If a student is distressed, pause, give them a pass and a quiet seat, and tell the teacher or the school’s support person.

The say-this lines are the lesson. Keep the 5, 8, 12, and 5 timing. Skip the extension if the clock is tight.
