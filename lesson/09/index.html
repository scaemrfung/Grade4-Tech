Mend the friendship

Students practise a sincere repair and learn to hear the difference between an apology and an excuse.

Grade
Grade 4
Subject
Technology infused into the host subject named below
Unit
Standing up and repairing harm

30 minutes

Regular classroom. No student devices.

Late January. Approximate only, about every two weeks from September to June.

For students

Today’s lesson

How can someone rebuild trust after hurting a friend online?

We are learning

  • We are learning what a sincere repair includes.
  • We are learning to spot an excuse that skips responsibility.

I can

  • I can say what happened and what the person can own.
  • I can tell a sincere apology from an excuse.

What to do

  • Decide if sorry is always enough. You may pass.
  • Read Cam’s draft.
  • Build a repair: name it, own it, make a fair step, check later.

Cam wants to repair it

This story is made up.

Cam wrote the comment “This looks like a baby made it” under Morgan’s drawing and watched others laugh. The next day Cam wants to fix the friendship. Cam’s first draft is: “Sorry, but you are too sensitive and it was just a joke.”

Check. Is this line sincere or an excuse: “Sorry, but it was just a joke”?

Learning intentions

  • We are learning what a sincere repair includes.
  • We are learning to spot an excuse that skips responsibility.

I can statements students see

  • I can say what happened and what the person can own.
  • I can tell a sincere apology from an excuse.

Success criteria / evidence of learning

  • Includes responsibility without “but you were too sensitive.”
  • Names a fair next step: delete or ask an adult to help remove the comment, tell the group the comment was unfair, and check in later.

Materials and prep

  • This page.
  • Board: Name it / Own it / Make a fair step / Check later.
  • Timer.

No Chromebooks, no student logins, and no device project. A projector is optional. The page is the lesson.

Room or space setup

  • Say clearly that nobody will be asked to apologize for a real incident in front of the class.

Lesson sequence (timed)

The four parts total 30 minutes: 5 + 8 + 12 + 5.

  1. Opening question · 5 min

    Say this. Is saying the word sorry always enough to repair a hurt? Thumbs up, down, or sideways. Keep any reason general.

    Do this. Land on: sorry is a start if it is sincere, and the actions after it matter.

  2. Explain and model · 8 min

    Say this. A repair can follow four steps. Name what happened. Own your part, with no excuse hiding in the word but. Make a fair next step. Check in later. Trust returns slowly. The person who was hurt does not have to accept the apology today. Common Sense teaches a routine called M.E.N.D. We are using our own four steps so the lesson stands on this page.

    Do this. Contrast: “Sorry, but it was just a joke” with “I wrote a mean comment about your drawing. That was unfair. I will delete it and tell the chat I was wrong.”

  3. Guided scenario · 12 min

    Say this. Help Cam repair the hurt to Morgan. Write or say the four steps. Do not act out Morgan crying. Keep it matter-of-fact.

    Do this. Pairs build the repair. Hear one. Ask: what should Cam do if Morgan does not want to talk yet?

  4. Check and close · 5 min

    Say this. Read either a sincere line or an excuse, and the class will show sincere or excuse with a hand signal we agree now.

    Do this. Use two lines from the answer guidance. Preview the media unit after the break in the year map.

If time is short. Do the contrast as a class and let pairs supply only the fair next step.

If you have extra time. Ask what “check later” sounds like a few days on: “I deleted it. I will not bring the joke up again. Are you all right to sit together at the table?”

Made-up scenario

Cam wants to repair it

Cam wrote the comment “This looks like a baby made it” under Morgan’s drawing and watched others laugh. The next day Cam wants to fix the friendship. Cam’s first draft is: “Sorry, but you are too sensitive and it was just a joke.”

  • What is wrong with the draft?
  • What could Cam say instead?
  • What fair steps come after the words?

Teacher answer guidance

  • The draft blames Morgan. “But” cancels the sorry. Calling it “just a joke” skips responsibility.
  • A sincere line: “I wrote a mean comment about your drawing. That was unfair. I am sorry.”
  • Fair steps: delete the comment or ask an adult to help, tell the group the comment was wrong, and check in later without demanding forgiveness today.
  • If Morgan does not want to talk, Cam respects that and still removes the comment.

Quick check. Is this line sincere or an excuse: “Sorry, but it was just a joke”?

Look for. It is an excuse. A sincere line owns the harm without blaming the other person.

Tech-free Plan B

The two sample lines on the board are enough if pairs need a closer model.

Support

Fill-in lines: “I did ___. It was unfair because ___. I will ___.”

Extension

Decide what Cam should not ask of Morgan, such as “tell everyone you forgive me today.” The extension stays inside the block. It is not homework and not a project.

Safety notes

Physical activity: not applicable. There is no gym task and no equipment to set out.

No forced apologies. No student plays the target in a dramatic scene.

Do not ask students for real names, addresses, schools, photos, usernames, passwords, screen-time diaries, or upsetting personal experiences. If a student discloses harm, follow the school’s safeguarding process. Do not investigate in front of the class.

Differentiation and inclusive supports

Offer the sincere line and the excuse line for sorting before asking a student to compose one.

Follow ISP supports listed in the class binder. Do not write medical details or student names into this public site or onto a slide you will publish.

Classroom management cues

If laughter returns, pause and reread the sincere line quietly. Do not pick the child who was most involved in a real conflict.

Assessment

A response with “but” that blames Morgan is not yet meeting the intention. Feedback: “You named the comment. Now own it without the excuse.” Students may reassess with one sincere sentence at the door or in Lesson 17’s private check if the skill is still shaky. Observations and the spoken product are enough.

Use observations, conversations, and products linked to the success criteria. Feedback describes what you noticed and one next step. There is no numeric or percent grade. If you use Elk Island Public Schools’ Grades 3–6 descriptive levels, the words are Not Yet Meeting Expectations, Minimally Meeting Expectations, Solidifying Expectations, and Mastering Expectations. A student can show the skill again in a flex lesson or in a one-minute conversation. Do not file personal stories.

Exit and clean-up

Do not post the apology online. Erase Morgan’s insult if it was rewritten. No names collected.

Sub notes

Do not run a class circle where students apologize to each other. Stay with Cam and Morgan.

The say-this lines are the lesson. Keep the 5, 8, 12, and 5 timing. Skip the extension if the clock is tight.
