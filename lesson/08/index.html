Learning intentions

  • We are learning how a single comment can grow when people forward, pile on, or stay silent.
  • We are reassessing the help-moves from last time.

I can statements students see

  • I can put the made-up events in an order that shows the harm growing.
  • I can point to one place where a helper could interrupt it.

Success criteria / evidence of learning

  • Orders at least three steps so the harm gets bigger, not smaller.
  • Names an interruption that uses a help-move rather than revenge.

Materials and prep

  • This page.
  • Four event strips on the board, out of order.
  • Timer.

No Chromebooks, no student logins, and no device project. A projector is optional. The page is the lesson.

Room or space setup

  • Keep Morgan’s story fictional. Remind the class of the four moves without asking for a real example.

Lesson sequence (timed)

The four parts total 30 minutes: 5 + 8 + 12 + 5.

  1. Opening question · 5 min

    Say this. Why might a mean comment feel bigger the next day than it did in the first minute? Use the idea, not a real event.

    Do this. Look for: more people saw it, it is still there, screenshots travel.

  2. Explain and model · 8 min

    Say this. Online meanness can escalate. Escalate means it grows. A comment can be copied, more people can add on, and the target can see it again and again. Silence can also let it grow. Interrupting early is kinder than trying to collect every copy later. You still should not become a new person doing harm.

    Do this. Show two steps only: one comment, then a screenshot in another chat. Ask what changed. The audience got bigger.

  3. Guided scenario · 12 min

    Say this. These four events are out of order. Put them in an order that shows the harm growing. Then mark the earliest place Ren could interrupt. This shows me what you remember. It is not a percent score.

    Do this. Pairs order the strips. Hear one order. Ask for the interruption.

  4. Check and close · 5 min

    Say this. What is one reason online meanness can grow, and one early interruption?

    Do this. If the interruption is missing, model it once more. Preview repairing the friendship.

If time is short. Order the events as a class. Pairs only choose the interruption.

If you have extra time. Ask what Ren should do if the comments have already spread. Look for: tell an adult, support Morgan privately, and do not keep forwarding.

Made-up scenario

Four events, out of order

Event cards, made up: A) Three more people add laughing comments the next day. B) One classmate writes “This looks like a baby made it” under Morgan’s dog drawing. C) Someone screenshots the comments into another chat. D) Morgan sees the screenshot again at recess and stops drawing.

  • What order shows the harm growing?
  • Where is the earliest interruption?
  • What move belongs there?

Teacher answer guidance

  • A sound order is B, C, A, D or B, A, C, D. The key is that B starts it and D shows the effect. C makes the audience bigger.
  • The earliest interruption is right after B: Ren does not laugh, checks on Morgan, and tells a trusted adult before the screenshot spreads.
  • Revenge, such as insulting the commenter in a new chat, is not the interruption we are practising.

Quick check. What is one reason the meanness can grow, and one early interruption?

Look for. It grows because more people see it, copy it, or add on. An early interruption is to refuse to pile on and tell a trusted adult, and to check on Morgan.

Tech-free Plan B

Read the four events and have the class vote on which comes first, second, third, and fourth.

Support

Number a correct order after a first try so a student can focus on the interruption.

Extension

Add a fifth made-up event that de-escalates the harm, such as an adult helping Morgan and the comment being removed. The extension stays inside the block. It is not homework and not a project.

Safety notes

Physical activity: not applicable. There is no gym task and no equipment to set out.

Do not invite “the worst thing you have seen.” Keep the strips mild.

Do not ask students for real names, addresses, schools, photos, usernames, passwords, screen-time diaries, or upsetting personal experiences. If a student discloses harm, follow the school’s safeguarding process. Do not investigate in front of the class.

Differentiation and inclusive supports

Give the events already in order to a student who needs the thinking load on the help-move only. That is still a valid reassessment of helping.

Follow ISP supports listed in the class binder. Do not write medical details or student names into this public site or onto a slide you will publish.

Classroom management cues

No contest for the most dramatic ending. Thank plain, safe answers.

Assessment

Reassessment of Lesson 7. Not Yet Meeting: the order shrinks the harm or the interruption is revenge. Minimally Meeting: order is right but no interruption. Solidifying: order and a help-move. Mastering: explains that the audience grew. Descriptive feedback only. A student may try the interruption sentence privately.

Use observations, conversations, and products linked to the success criteria. Feedback describes what you noticed and one next step. There is no numeric or percent grade. If you use Elk Island Public Schools’ Grades 3–6 descriptive levels, the words are Not Yet Meeting Expectations, Minimally Meeting Expectations, Solidifying Expectations, and Mastering Expectations. A student can show the skill again in a flex lesson or in a one-minute conversation. Do not file personal stories.

Exit and clean-up

Take the event strips down. Do not leave the insult posted.

Sub notes

Treat wrong orders as information, not a public correction that embarrasses. Model the early interruption if the class misses it.

The say-this lines are the lesson. Keep the 5, 8, 12, and 5 timing. Skip the extension if the clock is tight.
