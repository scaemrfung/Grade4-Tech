For students

Today’s lesson

How can we think about others when we share?

We are learning

  • We are learning that a reputation is the story people believe about someone.
  • We are learning that we are responsible for the part we publish.

I can

  • I can say how a post could change the story people believe about a teammate.
  • I can choose a fair action that does not publish the mistake.

What to do

  • Say who adds a chapter when someone posts.
  • Read Sasha’s choice.
  • Pick a fair action and a reason.

Learning intentions

  • We are learning that a reputation is the story people believe about someone.
  • We are learning that we are responsible for the part we publish.

I can statements students see

  • I can say how a post could change the story people believe about a teammate.
  • I can choose a fair action that does not publish the mistake.

Success criteria / evidence of learning

  • Refuses the post, or allows only a kind report to a coach in person.
  • Explains the effect on the teammate’s reputation, not only on likes.

Materials and prep

  • This page.
  • Board: Fair / Unfair to publish.
  • Timer.

No Chromebooks, no student logins, and no device project. A projector is optional. The page is the lesson.

Room or space setup

  • Link to last lesson in one sentence: some tracks are other people’s stories.

Lesson sequence (timed)

The four parts total 30 minutes: 5 + 8 + 12 + 5.

  1. Opening question · 5 min

    Say this. Reputation is the story people believe about a person. Who gets to add a chapter to that story when we post? Take two ideas. No real teammates.

    Do this. Land on: the poster adds a chapter, and the person in the post has to live with it.

  2. Explain and model · 8 min

    Say this. We are responsible for a fair and accurate picture of other people. A mistake in a game is a small moment. A post can make it the whole story. Asking permission matters, and even a yes does not make a mean post fair. A better share celebrates a repair: the teammate tried again. Private help, like telling a coach in person, is different from entertaining a chat.

    Do this. Model a fair line: “Great second try.” Model an unfair line: “Watch this miss forever.”

  3. Guided scenario · 12 min

    Say this. Decide what Sasha should do with the clip. Give a reason about reputation. Stay with the made-up team.

    Do this. Pairs choose. Require a reason. Hear a refusal and, if offered, the coach-in-person option. Do not play or mime a humiliating miss.

  4. Check and close · 5 min

    Say this. What is one fair choice for Sasha?

    Do this. Preview feelings and thinking traps. The unit shifts from what we publish to how tech habits feel.

If time is short. Choose fair or unfair as a class for three possible actions listed in the answer key.

If you have extra time. Ask what Sasha should say if friends insist “it is funny.” Look for a firm, kind refusal from Lesson 6.

Made-up scenario

The missed shot

Sasha filmed a made-up floor-hockey practice. A teammate missed an open net, then scored on the next try. Friends want Sasha to post only the miss with the caption “Watch this forever.” The teammate does not know the clip exists.

  • What story would that post tell?
  • What are Sasha’s fair choices?
  • Does a later “just kidding” fix the reputation?

Teacher answer guidance

  • The post would tell the story that the teammate is a joke, and hide the recovery. That is not a fair picture.
  • Fair choices: do not post the miss; ask before posting anything; if posting, use the second try and a kind caption; talk to a coach in person only if there is a real safety or teamwork need, not for laughs.
  • “Just kidding” later does not collect every copy and does not undo the first story people saw.

Quick check. What is one fair choice for Sasha?

Look for. Do not post the miss. Other fair choices: ask first, post the second try kindly, or speak to a coach in person if needed.

Tech-free Plan B

Read three possible actions. Thumbs up only for actions that are fair to publish.

Support

Sentence starter: “This would make people believe…”

Extension

Write the caption that would be fair if Sasha posts the second try instead. The extension stays inside the block. It is not homework and not a project.

Safety notes

Physical activity: not applicable. There is no gym task and no equipment to set out.

No real game clips, no naming a clumsy classmate, no slow-motion acting of a mistake.

Do not ask students for real names, addresses, schools, photos, usernames, passwords, screen-time diaries, or upsetting personal experiences. If a student discloses harm, follow the school’s safeguarding process. Do not investigate in front of the class.

Differentiation and inclusive supports

Offer the three actions as cards to sort before open talk.

Follow ISP supports listed in the class binder. Do not write medical details or student names into this public site or onto a slide you will publish.

Classroom management cues

Shut down laughter at the miss immediately and calmly. Return to the fair caption.

Assessment

A fair choice plus a reputation reason meets the intention. Posting because it will get likes does not. Feedback: “You protected the teammate. That is the responsibility.” This also reassesses Lesson 14’s permission idea.

Use observations, conversations, and products linked to the success criteria. Feedback describes what you noticed and one next step. There is no numeric or percent grade. If you use Elk Island Public Schools’ Grades 3–6 descriptive levels, the words are Not Yet Meeting Expectations, Minimally Meeting Expectations, Solidifying Expectations, and Mastering Expectations. A student can show the skill again in a flex lesson or in a one-minute conversation. Do not file personal stories.

Exit and clean-up

Erase unfair captions. Do not leave “watch this miss” on the board.

Sub notes

If the class cannot stay kind, stop the scenario and teach only the two model captions. Tell the classroom teacher.

The say-this lines are the lesson. Keep the 5, 8, 12, and 5 timing. Skip the extension if the clock is tight.
