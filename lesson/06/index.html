When the group pushes

A flex lesson. Students reassess clear messages and fair chats by coaching a character who is being pushed to send a silly photo.

For students

Today’s lesson

What can we do when a group pressures us online?

We are learning

  • We are reviewing how to refuse pressure without a mean reply.
  • We are checking that we still pause before a photo or a personal detail.

I can

  • I can name the pressure in a made-up chat.
  • I can give the character a short, firm, respectful reply and a next step.

What to do

  • Tell the difference between an invitation and pressure, using a made-up idea.
  • Read Devan’s chat.
  • Prepare a refusal and a next step.

Devan and the silly photo

This story is made up.

In a made-up chat, four classmates tell Devan: “Everyone sent a silly photo from their bedroom. If you do not send one, you are out of the group.” Devan does not want to send a photo of home.

Check. What is one firm, respectful sentence Devan can send?

Learning intentions

  • We are reviewing how to refuse pressure without a mean reply.
  • We are checking that we still pause before a photo or a personal detail.

I can statements students see

  • I can name the pressure in a made-up chat.
  • I can give the character a short, firm, respectful reply and a next step.

Success criteria / evidence of learning

  • Names the pressure: the group ties belonging to sending a photo.
  • Gives a refusal that does not insult the group, plus one safe next step such as leaving the chat or telling an adult.

Materials and prep

  • This page.
  • Board: Name it / Refuse / Next step.
  • Timer.

No Chromebooks, no student logins, and no device project. A projector is optional. The page is the lesson.

Room or space setup

  • Tell the class this is a review and reassessment, not a test score. Passing is allowed.

Lesson sequence (timed)

The four parts total 30 minutes: 5 + 8 + 12 + 5.

  1. Opening question · 5 min

    Say this. What is one difference between an invitation and pressure? Think of a made-up example, not a real group.

    Do this. Look for: an invitation can be declined safely; pressure threatens belonging.

  2. Explain and model · 8 min

    Say this. Online pressure can feel bigger because the messages arrive quickly and several people can see a refusal. A useful order is: name the pressure, refuse in a short respectful line, then choose a next step. Next steps include changing the subject, leaving, and telling a trusted adult if you feel unsafe or if they keep pushing. A photo of your face, your home, or your school sign is personal information. You do not owe the group that photo.

    Do this. Model: “I am not sending a photo. I can still play tomorrow.”

  3. Guided scenario · 12 min

    Say this. Coach Devan. Use name it, refuse, and next step. This is a chance to show what you remember from our privacy lessons and our chat lessons. Do not tell a real story.

    Do this. Pairs coach. Invite two rehearsals aloud. Note who meets both success criteria for your own reassessment notes, with no names on the board.

  4. Check and close · 5 min

    Say this. Say Devan’s refusal in one sentence.

    Do this. If many refusals are shaky, plan a one-minute redo at the start of Lesson 7. Preview standing up for someone else.

If time is short. Rehearse one refusal as a class and skip pair coaching.

If you have extra time. Ask what changes if the pressure is to exclude someone, linking back to Priya. The next step may be telling an adult sooner.

Made-up scenario

Devan and the silly photo

In a made-up chat, four classmates tell Devan: “Everyone sent a silly photo from their bedroom. If you do not send one, you are out of the group.” Devan does not want to send a photo of home.

  • What is the pressure?
  • What can Devan reply?
  • What is a safe next step if they keep pushing?

Teacher answer guidance

  • The pressure says belonging depends on sending a photo of home. That is not a fair invitation.
  • A firm reply: “I am not sending a photo. I can talk at school.”
  • If they keep pushing, Devan leaves the chat and tells a trusted adult. Sending the photo “just this once” is not the goal.
  • A reply such as “you are all losers” refuses, but it is not respectful. Ask for a cleaner line.

Quick check. What is one firm, respectful sentence Devan can send?

Look for. Any refusal that declines the photo without insulting the group. A next step is a bonus.

Tech-free Plan B

Students stand in pairs and say the refusal to a partner with voices low. No devices.

Support

Offer three refusal cards and let the student pick one and add a next step.

Extension

Write a second line Devan could send the next day that is friendly and still firm. The extension stays inside the block. It is not homework and not a project.

Safety notes

Physical activity: not applicable. There is no gym task and no equipment to set out.

No real photos, no “who is the pushy friend,” no dare to show a phone.

Do not ask students for real names, addresses, schools, photos, usernames, passwords, screen-time diaries, or upsetting personal experiences. If a student discloses harm, follow the school’s safeguarding process. Do not investigate in front of the class.

Differentiation and inclusive supports

A student may point to a card sequence instead of speaking in front of the class. Reassessment can be a private sentence to you.

Follow ISP supports listed in the class binder. Do not write medical details or student names into this public site or onto a slide you will publish.

Classroom management cues

Treat this as practice. Do not rank students aloud. Do not turn it into a debate about who has social media.

Assessment

This is a planned reassessment of Lessons 1 to 5: pausing on personal details, clear kind words, and including others. Use the success criteria. Descriptive levels, if you use them: Not Yet Meeting if the student tells Devan to send the photo; Minimally Meeting if they refuse but add an insult; Solidifying if they refuse kindly and name a next step; Mastering if they also explain why the photo is personal information. Offer a private redo. No percent grade.

Use observations, conversations, and products linked to the success criteria. Feedback describes what you noticed and one next step. There is no numeric or percent grade. If you use Elk Island Public Schools’ Grades 3–6 descriptive levels, the words are Not Yet Meeting Expectations, Minimally Meeting Expectations, Solidifying Expectations, and Mastering Expectations. A student can show the skill again in a flex lesson or in a one-minute conversation. Do not file personal stories.

Exit and clean-up

Erase refusals that include any real name. Thank the class for staying with the character.

Sub notes

Run it as coaching practice. If anyone is distressed, let them pass and tell the classroom teacher after, following school process.

The say-this lines are the lesson. Keep the 5, 8, 12, and 5 timing. Skip the extension if the clock is tight.
