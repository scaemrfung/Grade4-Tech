For students

Today’s lesson

What can a bystander do when meanness is digital?

We are learning

  • We are learning to recognize digital bullying.
  • We are learning help-moves that do not make the target more visible in a cruel way.

I can

  • I can say why the made-up comments are bullying.
  • I can choose a help-move: notice, check the person is okay, do not pile on, tell a trusted adult.

What to do

  • Offer one helper idea with no real names, or pass.
  • Read the story of Morgan’s drawing.
  • Choose two help-moves and one move to avoid.

Comments under Morgan’s drawing

This story is made up.

Morgan posts a made-up drawing of a dog. One classmate comments, “This looks like a baby made it.” Three more laughing comments appear that afternoon and again the next day. A student named Ren sees all of it and has not replied yet.

Check. What are two help-moves Ren can use?

Host subject

Physical Education and Wellness, Grade 4

Organizing idea. “Healthy Relationships: Personal well-being is supported through positive relationships built on communication, collaboration, empathy, and respect.”

Guiding question. “How can resolving conflict and healthy relationships be mutually supportive?”

Learning outcome. “Students reflect on resolution and explain connections to healthy relationships.”

Knowledge. “Bullying is repeated, deliberate, and targeted behaviour with intent to harm. Bullying behaviour can be seen or experienced verbally, socially, relationally, physically, digitally. Roles of individuals in situations of bullying include the bully, the bullied, and the bystander. Learning intentions

  • We are learning to recognize digital bullying.
  • We are learning help-moves that do not make the target more visible in a cruel way.

I can statements students see

  • I can say why the made-up comments are bullying.
  • I can choose a help-move: notice, check the person is okay, do not pile on, tell a trusted adult.

Success criteria / evidence of learning

  • Says the behaviour is repeated or targeted and meant to hurt, using the story.
  • Chooses at least two of the four help-moves and avoids a public pile-on against the first commenter.

Materials and prep

  • This page.
  • Four moves on the board.
  • Timer.

No Chromebooks, no student logins, and no device project. A projector is optional. The page is the lesson.

Room or space setup

  • Explain that nobody will be asked about a real bullying experience. Pass is available. You will not use the words as a joke.

Lesson sequence (timed)

The four parts total 30 minutes: 5 + 8 + 12 + 5.

  1. Opening question · 5 min

    Say this. If you saw a mean comment under a classmate’s drawing, what is one thing a helper might do? Use no real names.

    Do this. Take two safe ideas. Park revenge and public shaming to correct during the teach.

  2. Explain and model · 8 min

    Say this. Bullying is repeated, deliberate, and targeted behaviour with intent to harm. It can happen digitally. Roles include the person doing the harm, the person targeted, and the bystander. An upstander is a bystander who helps. Our four moves are: notice, check the person is okay, do not pile on, tell a trusted adult. Piling on against the commenter can become a new harm. Helping can be quiet.

    Do this. Write the four moves. Say that Common Sense teaches a framework called S.T.A.N.D.; today we are using our own four moves so we can teach without logging in.

  3. Guided scenario · 12 min

    Say this. Read about Morgan’s drawing. Decide which moves fit, and which move would make it worse. Stay with the story.

    Do this. Pairs choose. Hear two plans. Correct any plan that posts Morgan’s name with more jokes, even “supportive” jokes that quote the insult.

  4. Check and close · 5 min

    Say this. Name two help-moves for this story.

    Do this. Preview Lesson 8: we will watch how this kind of meanness can grow if nobody interrupts it.

If time is short. Teach the four moves and decide the plan as a whole class.

If you have extra time. Ask what “check the person is okay” sounds like in person: “I saw those comments. You did not deserve that. Do you want to come with me to tell someone?”

Made-up scenario

Comments under Morgan’s drawing

Morgan posts a made-up drawing of a dog. One classmate comments, “This looks like a baby made it.” Three more laughing comments appear that afternoon and again the next day. A student named Ren sees all of it and has not replied yet.

  • Is this bullying? How do you know?
  • Which two moves should Ren use?
  • What should Ren avoid?

Teacher answer guidance

  • Yes. The comments target Morgan’s work, they are meant to hurt, and they continue the next day. One rude comment is already unkind; the repeat makes the bullying description fit.
  • Ren can check on Morgan quietly, refuse to add a laughing comment, and tell a trusted adult. Ren might also say “That is not okay” if it feels safe.
  • Ren should not screenshot it to more chats, start a mean reply thread, or force Morgan to talk in front of others.

Quick check. What are two help-moves Ren can use?

Look for. Any two of: notice, check Morgan is okay, do not pile on, tell a trusted adult. A safe short refusal also counts.

Tech-free Plan B

Four paper signs around the room are unnecessary. Keep everyone seated and choose moves from the board so nobody is put on display.

Support

The four moves stay visible. Students may hold up fingers 1 to 4.

Extension

Decide who the trusted adult could be in the story: the teacher, a coach, or a family adult. There can be more than one. The extension stays inside the block. It is not homework and not a project.

Safety notes

Physical activity: not applicable. There is no gym task and no equipment to set out.

Do not ask who has been bullied. Do not show a real social media post. If a student discloses harm, thank them, stop the class share, and follow safeguarding.

Do not ask students for real names, addresses, schools, photos, usernames, passwords, screen-time diaries, or upsetting personal experiences. If a student discloses harm, follow the school’s safeguarding process. Do not investigate in front of the class.

Differentiation and inclusive supports

A student may work alone. Provide a card that already lists the four moves so the task is choosing, not memorizing under pressure.

Follow ISP supports listed in the class binder. Do not write medical details or student names into this public site or onto a slide you will publish.

Classroom management cues

No mean voices. No guessing which classmate is “like” the commenter. Reset quickly and calmly.

Assessment

Two fitting help-moves meet the intention. A plan that retaliates is not yet meeting it. Feedback: “You told an adult. The extra public post would put Morgan on display again.” Reassess in Lesson 8.

Use observations, conversations, and products linked to the success criteria. Feedback describes what you noticed and one next step. There is no numeric or percent grade. If you use Elk Island Public Schools’ Grades 3–6 descriptive levels, the words are Not Yet Meeting Expectations, Minimally Meeting Expectations, Solidifying Expectations, and Mastering Expectations. A student can show the skill again in a flex lesson or in a one-minute conversation. Do not file personal stories.

Exit and clean-up

Erase the insult from the board so it is not the last thing seen. Keep the four moves if you wish.

Sub notes

Read the safety note before the story. Do not ask for personal experiences. Report disclosures to the school’s usual person the same day.

The say-this lines are the lesson. Keep the 5, 8, 12, and 5 timing. Skip the extension if the clock is tight.
