Learning intentions

  • We are learning that some online actions are choices we can steer.
  • We are learning that copies and other people’s posts are harder to control.

I can statements students see

  • I can sort a made-up action into “we can steer this” or “hard to control.”
  • I can say why posting someone else’s result needs permission.

Success criteria / evidence of learning

  • Puts posting and asking permission on the steer side, and puts copies or someone else’s repost on the hard-to-control side.
  • Says the test-score post should not happen, because it is personal and unkind.

Materials and prep

  • This page.
  • Board: We can steer / Hard to control.
  • Timer.

No Chromebooks, no student logins, and no device project. A projector is optional. The page is the lesson.

Room or space setup

  • Define the phrase before the sort. No accounts, no real scores.

Lesson sequence (timed)

The four parts total 30 minutes: 5 + 8 + 12 + 5.

  1. Opening question · 5 min

    Say this. If you write something on paper and then photocopy it, can you get every copy back? Thumbs, then one reason.

    Do this. Use the photocopy idea as the bridge to online copies.

  2. Explain and model · 8 min

    Say this. An online track, often called a digital footprint, is the record left by what you open, type, and post, and also by what other people post about you. You can steer what you post, what you type, and whether you ask before you include someone else. You cannot fully steer copies, screenshots, or another person’s choice. Asking permission before you share someone else’s information is an ethical move. So is keeping the picture fair.

    Do this. Model: choosing not to post a friend’s photo is a steer. Once it is posted, a copy may remain. That is hard to control.

  3. Guided scenario · 12 min

    Say this. Sort the five actions. Then decide what should happen with the test-score idea. Made-up marks only. Nobody’s real mark.

    Do this. Pairs sort. Correct the idea that delete always erases every copy.

  4. Check and close · 5 min

    Say this. Name one thing we can steer and one thing that is hard to control.

    Do this. Preview reputation: the story other people believe about someone.

If time is short. Sort three actions as a class: posting, a screenshot by someone else, and asking first.

If you have extra time. Ask what a fair next step is if the score was already posted. Look for: delete, tell an adult, apologize using Lesson 9’s steps, and do not joke about it again.

Made-up scenario

Five actions and a quiz score

Made-up actions: 1) Choosing not to post. 2) Asking a friend before sharing their picture. 3) Another person screenshots your story. 4) Typing a comment you have not sent. 5) A classmate wants to post “Jordan got 2 out of 10 on the quiz” as a joke.

  • Which actions can we steer?
  • Which are hard to control?
  • What should happen with the quiz score?

Teacher answer guidance

  • We can steer 1, 2, and 4. They are choices before something is published.
  • Action 3 is hard to control because someone else already copied it.
  • Action 5 should not be posted. A quiz score is personal, and the joke is unfair. Jordan’s permission is required and would still not make a mean post kind.
  • Deleting later is incomplete. A copy may already exist.

Quick check. What is one online action we can steer, and one that is hard to control?

Look for. Steer: what we post, type, or ask permission for. Hard to control: screenshots, copies, or other people’s posts.

Tech-free Plan B

Five strips of paper in a pocket chart, or simply numbered lines on the board.

Support

Do the first action together so the two columns are clear.

Extension

Write the permission question: “May I tell the chat you finished the quiz?” and note that no is a full answer. The extension stays inside the block. It is not homework and not a project.

Safety notes

Physical activity: not applicable. There is no gym task and no equipment to set out.

Do not ask for real test scores, ranks, or who has posted about a classmate.

Do not ask students for real names, addresses, schools, photos, usernames, passwords, screen-time diaries, or upsetting personal experiences. If a student discloses harm, follow the school’s safeguarding process. Do not investigate in front of the class.

Differentiation and inclusive supports

A student may sort with arrows drawn for them and explain only the test-score line.

Follow ISP supports listed in the class binder. Do not write medical details or student names into this public site or onto a slide you will publish.

Classroom management cues

If a real score is spoken, do not repeat it. Say we are using a made-up quiz and move on.

Assessment

The sort plus the refusal to post the score meets the intention. Believing delete fixes everything is a partial understanding. Feedback: “You caught the unkind post. Remember a copy can remain.” Reassess inside Lesson 15.

Use observations, conversations, and products linked to the success criteria. Feedback describes what you noticed and one next step. There is no numeric or percent grade. If you use Elk Island Public Schools’ Grades 3–6 descriptive levels, the words are Not Yet Meeting Expectations, Minimally Meeting Expectations, Solidifying Expectations, and Mastering Expectations. A student can show the skill again in a flex lesson or in a one-minute conversation. Do not file personal stories.

Exit and clean-up

Shred strips. No photos of the sort if names were added; they should not have been.

Sub notes

Keep stressing made-up marks. Use the answer key if the columns get muddy.

The say-this lines are the lesson. Keep the 5, 8, 12, and 5 timing. Skip the extension if the clock is tight.
