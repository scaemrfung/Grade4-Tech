Swap the thought

A flex practice and reassessment. Students swap three made-up traps for fairer thoughts. This is not the Common Sense card game.

Grade
Grade 4
Subject
Technology infused into the host subject named below
Unit
Healthy tech habits
Time
30 minutes
Location
Regular classroom. No student devices.
Suggested block
Late May. Approximate only, about every two weeks from September to June.

For students

Today’s lesson

How can we practise a fairer thought?

We are learning

  • We are practising the swap from Lesson 16 on new examples.
  • We are showing the skill again so it can be reassessed.

I can

  • I can hear a trap and say a fairer thought.
  • I can keep the fairer thought believable.

What to do

  • Recall the three parts: trap, fairer thought, next step.
  • Swap the traps the teacher shows.
  • Keep characters made up.

Three traps to swap

This story is made up.

Trap 1, model: “The tablet is busy, so I never get a turn.” Trap 2: “I made one mistake in the class play, so the whole thing was ruined.” Trap 3: “They did not pick my idea for the poster, so my ideas are always bad.”

Check. What is a fairer thought for “the tablet is busy, so I never get a turn”?

Curriculum outcomes

Quoted wording is copied from the Grade 4 course on new LearnAlberta, retrieved 7 November 2026, or from the ICT program of studies named in the link. This lesson works toward the host outcome. It does not claim the outcome is fully achieved.

Host subject

Physical Education and Wellness, Grade 4

Organizing idea. “Character Development: Exploration of life opportunities and virtues develops resilience and personal talents and promotes lifelong learning.”

Guiding question. “How can a variety of life experiences influence resilience and perseverance?”

Learning outcome. “Students interpret how resilience and perseverance can be influenced by a variety of life experiences.”

Knowledge. “Strategies that support resilience include identifying a purpose, seeking positive role models, focusing on the solution instead of the challenge, breaking down tasks into smaller, achievable goals.”

Understanding. “Resilience may result in the increased ability to respond to future adversity. Resilience is supported by development of perseverance over time.”

Skills and procedures. “Describe strategies that support resilience.”

The outcome does not name technology or screen time. This lesson uses a made-up tech moment so students can describe a resilience strategy: focusing on the solution instead of the challenge.

ICT Division 2, working toward

Information and Communication Technology (K–12), 2000–2003

General outcome. “Students will understand the role of technology as it applies to self, work and society.”

Specific outcome. “2.4 assess the personal significance of having limitless access to information provided by communication networks, such as the Internet”

Information and Communication Technology is infused into the host subject. The class is working toward the Division 2 outcome below. This 30-minute block does not achieve it. The talk is about feelings and a planned pause. It is not an ergonomics or keyboarding lesson, and it does not collect a screen-time diary.

Learning intentions

  • We are practising the swap from Lesson 16 on new examples.
  • We are showing the skill again so it can be reassessed.

I can statements students see

  • I can hear a trap and say a fairer thought.
  • I can keep the fairer thought believable.

Success criteria / evidence of learning

  • Gives a fairer thought for at least two of the three traps.
  • The fairer thought stays kind to the character and does not deny all feelings.

Materials and prep

  • This page.
  • Three traps on the board, covered, then revealed one at a time.
  • Timer.

No Chromebooks, no student logins, and no device project. A projector is optional. The page is the lesson.

Room or space setup

  • Remind the class this is practice, not a contest and not a confession.

Lesson sequence (timed)

The four parts total 30 minutes: 5 + 8 + 12 + 5.

  1. Opening question · 5 min

    Say this. What are the three parts of our move? Look at the board if you need to. We will not keep score.

    Do this. Parts: name the trap, fairer thought, next step. Fill any gap quickly.

  2. Explain and model · 8 min

    Say this. Common Sense has a card game called Brain Battle for this skill. We are not using their cards. Our practice is oral. I will read a trap. You will swap it for a thought a Grade 4 student could actually believe. A fairer thought can still admit disappointment. It just does not jump to “always” or “nobody.”

    Do this. Model trap 1 together before pairs try trap 2 and 3.

  3. Guided scenario · 12 min

    Say this. For each remaining trap, write or say a fairer thought and a next step. Made-up characters only. This is a second chance to show the skill.

    Do this. Work through trap 2 and trap 3. Collect one swap each. Note, privately, who shows the success criteria.

  4. Check and close · 5 min

    Say this. Give me the swap for the tablet trap, in one sentence.

    Do this. Preview the last lesson: habits when media stirs a feeling.

If time is short. Do all three as a class. Pairs whisper only the last swap.

If you have extra time. Invite one student to invent a mild trap for the class to swap, still fictional, and veto anything personal or mean.

Made-up scenario

Three traps to swap

Trap 1, model: “The tablet is busy, so I never get a turn.” Trap 2: “I made one mistake in the class play, so the whole thing was ruined.” Trap 3: “They did not pick my idea for the poster, so my ideas are always bad.”

  • What is a believable fairer thought?
  • What is a small next step?
  • Which words are traps: never, always, ruined?

Teacher answer guidance

  • Trap 1: “The tablet is busy now. I can ask when my turn is.” Next step: ask the teacher for the order.
  • Trap 2: “I made a mistake and the play still finished. I can practise that line.” Next step: say the line once tonight or ask to try it tomorrow.
  • Trap 3: “They chose another idea this time. I can offer a new idea later.” Next step: write one new idea on scrap paper, no name required.
  • “I should not feel disappointed” is not the goal. Disappointment can stay. The always or never is the trap.

Quick check. What is a fairer thought for “the tablet is busy, so I never get a turn”?

Look for. The tablet is busy now, and I can ask about my turn. “Never” is the trap.

Tech-free Plan B

Read the traps. The class choruses a fairer thought after think-time. No cards to prepare.

Support

A word bank: maybe, not yet, one time, I can ask, I can try one small thing.

Extension

Add a next step to a swap that only had a thought. The extension stays inside the block. It is not homework and not a project.

Safety notes

Physical activity: not applicable. There is no gym task and no equipment to set out.

Veto traps about bodies, families, grades, or real classmates. Have a spare mild trap ready.

Do not ask students for real names, addresses, schools, photos, usernames, passwords, screen-time diaries, or upsetting personal experiences. If a student discloses harm, follow the school’s safeguarding process. Do not investigate in front of the class.

Differentiation and inclusive supports

A student may choose between two fairer thoughts you supply. That still shows discrimination.

Follow ISP supports listed in the class binder. Do not write medical details or student names into this public site or onto a slide you will publish.

Classroom management cues

No points, no speed round that rewards shouting. Silent think time first.

Assessment

Reassessment of Lesson 16. Not Yet Meeting: repeats the trap or insults the character. Minimally Meeting: a kinder thought that is still absolute, such as “nothing bad ever happens.” Solidifying: a believable swap. Mastering: swap plus a useful next step. Descriptive feedback. Private redo of one trap is enough. No percent.

Use observations, conversations, and products linked to the success criteria. Feedback describes what you noticed and one next step. There is no numeric or percent grade. If you use Elk Island Public Schools’ Grades 3–6 descriptive levels, the words are Not Yet Meeting Expectations, Minimally Meeting Expectations, Solidifying Expectations, and Mastering Expectations. A student can show the skill again in a flex lesson or in a one-minute conversation. Do not file personal stories.

Exit and clean-up

Erase traps and leave one fairer thought visible until dismissal, then clear the board.

Sub notes

Use only the three traps on this page. Do not invite darkest thoughts.

The say-this lines are the lesson. Keep the 5, 8, 12, and 5 timing. Skip the extension if the clock is tight.
