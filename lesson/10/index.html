How media aims at feelings

Students study a made-up game ad and name the feeling it aims for and the tricks that create it.

Curriculum outcomes

Quoted wording is copied from the Grade 4 course on new LearnAlberta, retrieved 7 November 2026, or from the ICT program of studies named in the link. This lesson works toward the host outcome. It does not claim the outcome is fully achieved.

Host subject

English Language Arts and Literature, Grade 4

Organizing idea. “Text Forms and Structures: Identifying and applying text forms and structures improves understanding of content, literary style, and our rich language traditions.”

Guiding question. “How can text organization influence communication?”

Learning outcome. “Students examine how the form and structure of texts can support the communication of ideas and information.”

Knowledge. “Texts can be communicated for a variety of purposes, including to inform, persuade, provide enjoyment. Media texts can be digital or non-digital and can combine sounds, words, images, and graphics.”

Understanding. “Text form or structure can support the enjoyment and communication of ideas and information for a variety of purposes.”

Skills and procedures. “Confirm the author’s or text creator’s purpose based on information in the text.”

The full Grade 4 knowledge statement also includes genre, literary forms, and narrative structure. Those parts are not the target today.

ICT Division 2, working toward

Information and Communication Technology (K–12), 2000–2003

General outcome. “Students will become discerning consumers of mass media and electronic information.”

Specific outcome. “2.1 recognize that graphics, video and sound enhance communication”

Specific outcome. “2.2 describe how the use of various texts and graphics can alter perception”

Information and Communication Technology is infused into the host subject. The class is working toward the Division 2 outcome below. This 30-minute block does not achieve it. The class studies a short made-up text written on the board. No video is required.

Learning intentions

  • We are learning that media is made on purpose.
  • We are learning to name a feeling a text aims for, and one trick it uses.

I can statements students see

  • I can name a feeling the made-up ad wants.
  • I can point to one choice the creator made, such as the words, the crowd, or the dare.

Success criteria / evidence of learning

  • Names a plausible feeling, such as excitement, worry about being left out, or a wish to win.
  • Links that feeling to a specific part of the ad.

Materials and prep

  • This page.
  • Board: Feeling / Creator’s choice.
  • Timer.

No Chromebooks, no student logins, and no device project. A projector is optional. The page is the lesson.

Room or space setup

  • Say that students will not be asked what media has upset them. They may pass.

Lesson sequence (timed)

The four parts total 30 minutes: 5 + 8 + 12 + 5.

  1. Opening question · 5 min

    Say this. Can a poster make a person feel excited without the person choosing that feeling first? Thumbs, then one reason. No show or game titles from home.

    Do this. Accept yes, because music, faces, and words are chosen by a creator.

  2. Explain and model · 8 min

    Say this. A media text can be digital or not, and it can mix words and pictures. Creators have a purpose. One purpose is to persuade, and feelings help them do it. A feeling is information, not an order. If an ad makes you feel left out, you can notice the trick and still choose. We will not share a time media really upset you.

    Do this. Model a one-line cereal box: “Champions pour this.” Feeling: wanting to be a champion. Choice: the word champions.

  3. Guided scenario · 12 min

    Say this. Read the made-up game ad. With a partner, name the feeling and the creator’s choice. Then say one thought a player could use so the feeling is not the boss.

    Do this. Hear two pairs. Keep real game titles off the board.

  4. Check and close · 5 min

    Say this. What feeling does the ad want, and what is one choice that creates it?

    Do this. Preview checking whether a claim is even true.

If time is short. Do the ad together. Skip the “thought a player could use” until the close, and make it your model.

If you have extra time. Ask whether the ad informs, persuades, or entertains. Persuade should be the main purpose. Lesson 12 will slow this down.

Made-up scenario

The “Only winners” ad

Made-up ad: A picture of cheering kids, a glowing controller, and the words “Only winners play SkyDash. Losers log off. Your friends are already in.” There is no price and no real company.

  • What feeling does this aim for?
  • Which choice creates that feeling?
  • What could a player tell themselves?

Teacher answer guidance

  • Feelings that fit: excitement, fear of being left out, or shame about losing.
  • Choices: the cheering crowd, the word losers, and “your friends are already in.”
  • A steady thought: “This ad wants me to feel rushed. I can decide later without calling myself a loser.”

Quick check. What feeling does the ad want, and which words help create it?

Look for. A fitting feeling linked to a real line in the ad, especially “only winners,” “losers,” or “friends are already in.”

Tech-free Plan B

Read the ad twice. Students tap the desk once when they hear a feeling-word.

Support

Offer a feeling bank: excited, left out, curious, annoyed, brave. Students may choose from the bank.

Extension

Change one line so the ad informs instead of pressuring. For example: “This game has ten levels.” The extension stays inside the block. It is not homework and not a project.

Safety notes

Physical activity: not applicable. There is no gym task and no equipment to set out.

If a student looks upset, let them pass and offer a quiet job such as timekeeper. Do not ask them to explain.

Do not ask students for real names, addresses, schools, photos, usernames, passwords, screen-time diaries, or upsetting personal experiences. If a student discloses harm, follow the school’s safeguarding process. Do not investigate in front of the class.

Differentiation and inclusive supports

A student may draw an arrow from a feeling word to a line in the ad instead of speaking.

Follow ISP supports listed in the class binder. Do not write medical details or student names into this public site or onto a slide you will publish.

Classroom management cues

No contest about who plays the most games. No mocking a feeling.

Assessment

Feeling plus a specific line meets the intention. “It is cool” is a start; ask “which words?” Feedback stays on the ad. Reassess in Lesson 12.

Use observations, conversations, and products linked to the success criteria. Feedback describes what you noticed and one next step. There is no numeric or percent grade. If you use Elk Island Public Schools’ Grades 3–6 descriptive levels, the words are Not Yet Meeting Expectations, Minimally Meeting Expectations, Solidifying Expectations, and Mastering Expectations. A student can show the skill again in a flex lesson or in a one-minute conversation. Do not file personal stories.

Exit and clean-up

Erase any real title a student called out, with a neutral “we are staying with the made-up ad.”

Sub notes

Do not play a video. The printed ad is the text. Pass is success-neutral.

The say-this lines are the lesson. Keep the 5, 8, 12, and 5 timing. Skip the extension if the clock is tight.
